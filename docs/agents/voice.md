# Voice and claims for site copy

Site copy should sound like David wrote it and promise only what he can back up in an interview. Four leading words carry this guide: **plain**, **specific**, **understated**, **traceable**.

## Steps

1. **Gather facts.** Pull every claim, number, title, and date from `Resume-David-Dangerfield.pdf`, his LinkedIn profile, `career-history.md` in his personal-history repo, or something David confirmed in writing. Other private notes are for learning his style, not for facts, unless he approves a specific fact.
2. **Draft in his voice** (see Voice).
3. **Shape it for a skim** (see Structure).
4. **Check it.** Done means all of these are true:
   - Every claim and number traces to a source from step 1.
   - Every sentence would sound natural if David said it out loud to a hiring manager.
   - `npm run validate:source` passes.

## Voice

- **Plain.** Everyday words, short declarative sentences, first person. Say the thing directly: "I rewrote the bill-pay frontend," not "I spearheaded a transformative modernization initiative."
- **Specific.** Names, numbers, tools, and what actually happened. A concrete detail ("desktop-only AngularJS, rewritten as a responsive Vue and TypeScript app") carries the weight an adjective would only claim.
- **Understated.** Let facts imply quality. Describe what happened, never what kind of person David is. Adjectives about David himself (expert, passionate, trusted) are out; replace each with the fact that would earn it. Keep the tone calm and matter-of-fact, with no performed excitement about roles or technology.
- **Honest about tradeoffs.** He names what didn't work, what he'd do differently, and when something is only right for one situation. Small contributions get left out, not inflated.
- **Credits others.** "We" for team work, "I" for his own decisions and code.
- **Brief.** Give a taste and link to the resume for the rest.
- **Dry humor, sparingly.** An occasional wry aside in parentheses is his style. Keep headings, the hero, and calls to action straight.
- **Punctuation.** Commas, colons, periods, and parentheses. Em dashes are a hard ban everywhere (the validator enforces it in site copy). Professional copy uses no exclamation points.

## Claims

- **Traceable** is the bar: if a sentence can't be traced to a source from step 1, cut it or ask David.
- **Level:** the headline title is "Senior / Staff Frontend Engineer", his target level. Past titles, employers, and dates appear exactly as on the resume. Use the resume's verb for what he did.
- **Frontend first, full-stack welcome:** frontend is the headline, and he's also open to full-stack roles. His backend work is real experience (most recently .NET/C# at Patterson), not a footnote.
- **Featured roles:** when only three fit, feature Patterson Companies, Life Time, and NextEra Analytics. Nurocor was a part-time side role; mention it in supporting lists.
- **Volume metrics:** describe what the work did rather than counting it. Skip PR and repo counts, and any other volume number that could read as too high or too low; the resume's "20+ repositories" is the one exception.
- **Availability and location:** available now for senior or staff roles, frontend or full-stack, full-time or contract. Based in Minneapolis. Open to remote, hybrid, and on-site roles.
- **Engagement type:** full-time, or an hourly contract. For contracts he bills through his LLC, so say "corp-to-corp available". Say "contract", not "consulting". The LLC's name and address stay off the site.
- **AI tools:** mention at most once per page, concretely (GitHub Copilot and Claude in his daily work; this site is built and maintained with them).
- **"20+ years":** at most once per page.

## Structure

This is the marketer's part, and it serves the reader rather than selling:

- Lead with what a recruiter decides on: role and level, availability, location, and how to reach him.
- Headings state a fact or a result, not a slogan.
- Outcome first, then how, then the stack.
- One idea per section. Link to the resume instead of repeating it.
- The 20-second test: a recruiter skimming the home page leaves knowing his role and level, that he's available for full-time or contract work and where, two or three shipped outcomes with numbers, and that resume, email, LinkedIn, and booking are one click away.
